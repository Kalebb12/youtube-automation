Motion: camera moves, parallax, crossfades#

Every scene is a still image, but none of them sits still. assemble.py (using motion.py) turns each image into a slow camera move with depth parallax, crossfades between scenes, and fades the whole video in from black and out to black. It all runs on your laptop CPU - no GPU, no cost.

What you get#

  • Depth parallax ("2.5D"). A small depth model (Depth Anything V2 Small, Apache 2.0, ~100 MB, downloaded once into models/) works out what is near and far in each image. As the camera drifts, near things (the three men) move more than the background (the furnace), so the still reads as a real camera move. Depth maps are cached in images/<story>/depth/.
  • Eased camera moves. Each scene gets one of zoom_in, pan_right, zoom_out, pan_left, in that cycle so no two neighbours match. Moves start and stop gently (smoothstep).
  • Crossfades between scenes instead of hard cuts. Narration timing is unchanged: the next image starts fading in exactly at the scene boundary.
  • Fade in / fade out of the whole video.

Speed: about 12 s of CPU per 5 s of video, so ~2-3 minutes for a Short (first run adds ~5 s per image for depth, plus the one-time model download).

Settings#

Channel profile "motion" < story "motion" < command-line flags. A scene can pin its move.

Setting Bible default Meaning
parallax true Depth parallax on. false = flat Ken Burns.
strength 1.0 Parallax amount. 0.5 subtle, 1.5 strong. Too high shows warping at edges.
zoom 0.08 How far zoom moves travel (8%).
transition "fade" Crossfade style - any ffmpeg xfade transition: fade, dissolve, fadeblack, fadewhite, smoothleft, smoothright, circleopen, radial, ...
transition_s 0.7 Crossfade length (s). 0 = hard cuts.
fade_in 0.6 Fade from black at the start (s).
fade_out 1.2 Fade to black at the end (s).

Horror uses stronger parallax (1.3), fadeblack transitions and slower fades.

Per scene, in scenes/<story>.json:

{"id": 5, "move": "zoom_in", ...}       // zoom_in | zoom_out | pan_left | pan_right

Per story:

"motion": {"strength": 1.4, "transition": "dissolve", "transition_s": 1.0}

One-off from the command line:

python assemble.py fiery_furnace --strength 1.4 --transition dissolve --transition-s 1.0
python assemble.py fiery_furnace --flat            # no parallax

Why not AI video (Wan 2.2)?#

We checked: Wan2.2's only model in reach (TI2V-5B) needs ~24 GB of GPU memory even with offloading, against the free Kaggle T4's 16 GB, and takes ~9 minutes per 5-second clip on an RTX 4090 - very roughly an hour or more per clip on a T4, i.e. most of a week's free GPU quota per video. The 14B image-to-video model is 126 GB. Depth parallax gives most of the "moving shot" feel for free. It remains a possible experiment for the hook scene only.
