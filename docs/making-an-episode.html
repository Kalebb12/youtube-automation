Making an episode, start to finish#

Worked example (a real run): Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in the fiery furnace (Daniel 3:13-28) on the Bible channel.

Step Where Time it took
1. Draft laptop → Claude 51 s
2. Review + approve you -
3. Images (6, FLUX.2 klein) Kaggle T4 10.5 min (~90 s per image) + queue
4. Narration laptop ~1.5 min
5. Video laptop ~4-5 min

One-time setup before your first episode: see Overview & setup, including python run_kaggle.py --setup-model (caches the image model on Kaggle).

1. Draft the script#

source .venv/bin/activate
python script.py --provider claude --channel bible \
    --passage "Daniel 3:13-28" \
    --brief "Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego refuse to bow to Nebuchadnezzar's golden image, are thrown into the fiery furnace heated seven times hotter, and a fourth figure walks with them in the fire; they come out unburned" \
    --id fiery_furnace

What happens:

  1. The passage is fetched from bible-api.com in the channel's translation (KJV).
  2. The model (Claude Opus 5.5 here; Gemini if you leave out --provider) writes the story in the channel's narrator voice: hook first, storytelling (not verse-by-verse), and for each scene a structured shot - subject, action, setting, camera, lighting - planned for the channel's image model (see images).
  3. The draft is checked: placeholders point at real characters, quotes are marked and appear word for word in the passage, the narration fits the channel's target_duration_s, and the image rules hold (characters per image, nothing a camera can't see, varied cameras, prompt length). Problems go back to the model with the exact issue (up to 3 tries).
  4. scenes/fiery_furnace.json is written with "status": "draft".

Which provider? Gemini is free but its free tier is often overloaded (503) - script.py retries for ~2 minutes, then gives up. Claude costs about $0.05-0.10 per draft and answers in under a minute. Put LLM_PROVIDER=claude in .env to make it the default.

2. Review it (required)#

Open scenes/fiery_furnace.json next to the passage. Check:

  • [ ] Every event, speaker and detail is in the passage. Nothing invented (motives, dialogue, names, numbers, doctrine).
  • [ ] The hook (scene 1) is drawn from the story, not a question to the viewer.
  • [ ] Anything in quotation marks is exact public-domain text and has a matching quote field (here: "But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods", 3:18).
  • [ ] Characters have purely visual descriptions that are easy to tell apart.
  • [ ] Each image_prompt shows this scene's key moment, named characters in the foreground, extras in the background. Sensitive figures follow the channel rules (here the fourth man in the fire is a plain silhouette - no halo).
  • [ ] Narration reads well aloud. Kokoro can stumble on archaic words - spell them how they sound if needed.

Edit anything you like, then set "status": "approved". Nothing reaches the GPU until you do.

3. Render the images (Kaggle GPU)#

python run_kaggle.py fiery_furnace      # or no name: every approved story
  • Uploads the approved story plus the channel profiles, attaches the cached image model, starts the GPU job, polls every 30 s, then downloads images/fiery_furnace/scene_01.png ....
  • FLUX.2 klein on the T4: ~90 s to load, ~90 s per image (bf16 is emulated on the T4).
  • If it sits in queued for a long time, see troubleshooting.

Look at the images. To redo one, add "seed": 555 (any number) to that scene, or adjust its image_prompt, and run again.

4. Narration#

python voice.py fiery_furnace

Speaks each scene with the channel's voice (Bible: bm_george, speed 0.95) into images/fiery_furnace/audio/. Prints the total length against the channel target. Cached: only scenes whose text or voice changed are re-spoken.

5. Video#

python assemble.py fiery_furnace

Each image stays up for its narration plus a short pause, as a slow eased camera move with depth parallax (see motion), crossfading into the next scene; the whole video fades in from and out to black. Subtitles are burned in (balanced chunks, clear of the Shorts UI), music from music/bible/ is ducked under the voice, and the mix is levelled to -16 LUFS. Output: images/fiery_furnace/fiery_furnace.mp4 (1080x1920, ~55 s, ~22 MB).

To pick the music first, audition it in seconds (see music):

python music.py preview fiery_furnace --volume-db -16      # → images/fiery_furnace/music_preview.mp3
python music.py set fiery_furnace --volume-db -16          # keep it

6. Watch it before it goes anywhere#

Every video gets watched in full before publishing: pronunciation, subtitle timing, any image that contradicts the narration.

Fixing things later#

You changed... Re-run
narration text voice.py + assemble.py (no GPU)
an image prompt, seed, character description run_kaggle.py + assemble.py (voice is cached)
the voice or speed voice.py + assemble.py
motion (parallax, transitions, fades) assemble.py (depth maps are cached)
music music.py preview to audition, music.py set, then assemble.py

voice.py and assemble.py warn you if image prompts changed since the images were rendered.

Series: the next part#

For a multi-part story, draft each later part with --continue-from <previous id>: the previous episode's characters are reused word for word (so people look the same across episodes) and its narration is given as "previously on", so the new part doesn't repeat it.

python script.py --provider claude --channel bible --passage "Genesis 37:25-36" \
    --continue-from joseph_part2 --brief "Joseph and His Brothers (Part 3): sold to the Ishmeelites"
